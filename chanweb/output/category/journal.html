The FIRST Presidential Fitness Challenge

Posted on: Mon 15 September 2025

It was Sunday on the hottest day of a recent heatwave. Twelve of my unsuspecting closest friends arrived at a local park expecting "Field Games and BBQ." Meanwhile, I was expecting a revolt as soon as I revelaed my loosely veiled aspiration to relive on of the more humiliating experiences of our mandatory Phys.Ed classes in school - the Presidential Fitness Challenge. The goal wasn't to relive the humiliation, but rather to have a structured out-of-doors event that I could repeat year after year.

To the one unitiated adult friend of the group (him being of European nationality), I explained it like this:

In the 80s and 90s in the Unite States, we had something called the Presidential Fitness Challenge where we had some "standard" activities and expectations to be considered fit. I did not tell everyone [attending (sic.)] that the "field" part is this test. Many people have negative experiences in elementary school doing these. So please keep this secret.

The two uninitiated nephews were led to believe you cannot be president without meeting our current president's physical capabilities. No one bothered to correct this misinformation as it later became a bit of a running joke.

Somewhere among the group after the great reveal, I heard the word "redemption" here and there. It really was! Many participants surprised themselves with their current capabilities having exceeded their personal benchmarks set half-their-age-or-more ago, although, perhaps with some residual soreness over the next couple of days.

While performance was the general goal of my guests, making sure everyone had fun was mine. Three and a half hours in the morning sun and six hours in the glow of a few cold bubbly drinks later, I would say we all hit our personal targets and could call the weekend well spent!

Xanga Digest Emails

Posted on: Thu 12 December 2024

I am keeping the old emails from Xanga Digest from a thousand years ago.

It is really interesting to read the thoughts of my peers from our most stressful and vulnerable years. At least, they were the first times we experienced the amount of pain we could self-inflict via obligation.

What I am trying to say is that, as adults, there's a lot of pressure with real world consequences that we didn't even sign up for. The pressure comes from other people (like coworkers), non-human entities (like banks), and societal pressure (like social media.) But in high school, the pressure often does not result in real-world consequences. Let's say you decided not to audition for that concert ensemble? Would you still go to college? Yes. Would you still have friends? Yes. Well, then, exactly what would really change? Nothing. Maybe you'd save some $$ or re-allocate that time to another type of art.

It is really wonderful to read these raw accounts of people I once cared for because it reflects a time when us millenials weren't so afraid. As teens, we wanted to be seen so badly that most of us put some pretty gnarly and personal things openly on the internet. One just had to have your link (or a bad habit of not deleting emails.) As adults, we want so badly to be seen a certain WAY which is such a cruel nuance.

FUTURE LIZ EDIT HERE: I found this entries sitting in my Freewrite and am really interested in where I was going here. I think I had a lot of hesitation about sharing my authentic personality online. I still do.

But lately (it's almost a year since this was originally written,) I've been thinking about how much I appreciate the personal blog.

I recently found blogroll.org and subscribed to some random people's online journals. They all have a disclaimer that their website is unrefined. But honestly, that's what I am looking for - no disclaimer needed!

It's been tough to find a place where people are authentic on the internet. I suppose, like those I started to subscribe to, I can also be a part of the internet I want to see. Democratizing a space isn't about wishing, it's about contributing as well. OKAY FUTURE LIZ EDIT OVER.

Mental Wellness and "Soma"

Posted on: Mon 09 December 2024

Today, I took a mental wellness day. For some reason, the idea of showing up for work felt repulsive. If there is ever a good time to listen to your heart, it is when there is this acute of a feeling.

I spent the day catching up on the pile of work that accrued for my personal life. It's hard to imagine that at some point in my life, I had ample time to do my day job and have room in my mind to imagine up other projects - and actually do them.

I sometimes believe that the American culture of constant hustling sets us back from higher levels of thinking because of the decision fatigue, brain-drain, soul-sucking that people often attribute to "job." Higher levels of thinking can include being a good friend or partner, taking up a hobby, exploring other aspects of life to discover what is actually meaningful to you, etc.

Have you ever read "A Brave New World" by Alduous Huxley? I don't remember much, but what I do remember is the soma. Soma was a substance that felt good, was taken by everyone (normalized), and muted the thoughts that took people from neutral and complacent to inspired and curious.

In writing this, I wonder if, as adults, we are so filled with obligation that we no longer have capacity for the hard work of curiosity. There are so many blogs that talk about "boredom breeds creativity" and that we are so preoccupied by our phones/jobs/the-algorithm that we are not who we want to be.

... I'm not sure where I am going with this. Just expressing a current state of mind, I suppose.

Anyways, I made an email sorting program that will help me reduce my gmail space after years of archiving versus deleting garbage emails. I wanted to write about that, but got distracted because I have a bit of guilt about taking a mental health day when I know I shouldn't feel guilty about that.

Finally! A FREEWRITE!

Posted on: Mon 02 September 2024

This is my first entry using the Freewrite. After 5 years of following this product, I finally have my hands on one!

The journey started a bit rough when I received this from eBay - a resale market on the internet. I noticed that the device was not able to charge! M suggested that I email the manufacturer to understand how much it would cost to have this device repaired - specifically with a new battery. This way, I could get an estimate of how to best decide cost versus benefit. I am so glad he made that suggestion because I got a response! Not only do they not take repairs, but there's something about a USB-A to USB-C connection that is required for the device to charge.

Well, as you can see, the Freewrite was able to receive a charge and we are now in business.

Instead of purchasing this for $500 (as an early backer) or $649, I got this device for $240 (including shipping.) I figured it would be a low-risk experiment and held onto that logic as the anguish of a potentially mal-functioning battery wracked my brain. I will admit that a part of me was convinced to just get a brand new device and return this one via the eBay Customer Protection policy.

I am so happy this is working! I hope to use it as a way to keep my blog alive - sharing little tidbits of my day in a fun and informal format.

Okay, onto the tidbits...

THOUGHTS ON ARCHIVING INSTAGRAM PHOTOS I have a lot of photos on my Instagram. It is a record of my growth from college freshman to early-30's adult (as of today.) I like my old photos because they were OF A TIME. Now? It seems like people are really focused on a highly curated version of their life. The funny thing is - I was much more adventurous as a young person than I am now. Maybe I want to keep those old photos up as a record of who I used to be.

SLEEPING GODS GAME

I picked up this game because of its easy-to-stop-and-pick-up structure. I guess another way to put it is that it is a legacy game. The mechanics have some DnD-esque parts to it, such as the concept of initiative and armour classes. But it levels the playing field by giving characters a guaranteed baseline of probable success.

Playing with M was great since we agreed to do the walk through after a bit of light studying at lunch. We finished the tutorial and will pick it up again next time.

The only annoying part was packing up the game because 1) I was running out of steam and 2) there never seems to be enough baggies for all the little pieces.

Building a Study Schedule

Posted on: Fri 11 August 2023

When working from books to learn something new, I know that the author has a learning plan set which is commonly found in the introduction or an "About this Book" section. The issue for self-guided learning is understanding how that book's structure fits into my current ability to read/concentrate on this topic week-on-week.

The goal of this article is to break down book-guided learning into consumable pieces using Google Sheets and a bit of automation.

By breaking down books into pieces that both follow the author's intended learning structure and respect my personal constraints, I am set up for continuous forward motion on multiple pursuits. Sure, progress at times may feel slow, but forward motion is always a positive gain.

Some of the constraints I deal with are - time, attention span, and fear of that "stuck" feeling where I am just not progressing on my project aspirations. (More constraints are best explained in my Learn to Study Guide.)

To respect these constraints, I designed a reading schedule that: - Sets weekly reading assignments (not monthly or daily) - Alerts me when the study load is going beyond my upper bound or is highly optimistic during times of ... you know... life.

So, how can I make something that gives me this information? Google Sheets!

When I first set up this spreadsheet (V1 and V2), it was painstakingly manual and, worse, it was inflexible. I had the most friction with:

  • Getting week start dates (other than looking at a calendar)
  • Manually entering start and finish reading position for each chapter (even with some clunky formulas)
  • Re-working the entire manual process when I needed to make customizations
  • New years, new books required a repeat of this slow-and-painful data entry process making me question my goals in life

After 2-3 years of tinkering, I landed on a data model-inspired ecosystem and used the power of sheet =QUERY() and data validation to semi-automate this process.

Key features:

  • Easily pick any book from desired interests with a one-time set up
  • Drop down menus to assign chapters with automated population of chapter attributes (such as start, end pages)
  • Drop down book selection
  • Week of Year (WoY) implementation to simplify week-start date settings

At a high-level, set up for this type of study guide schedule is quite simple and does not require an advance degree in excel.

Skills required:

  • =CONCAT()
  • =VLOOKUP()
  • =QUERY()
  • Data Entry and patience (but only one time per book!)

Steps:

  1. Set up a dates sheet that has the columns: nixes, week of year, dates, day of week
  2. Set up book details with columns: index, title, chapter, start, finish, total pages, ...
  3. Use VLOOKUP to reference dates based on dates.index = CONCAT(week of year, date, day of week)
  4. Set data validation (which automatically filters to distinct values in a column) for Book Title (row 1:1), Chapter, etc.
  5. Use =QUERY() on schedule sheet some rows down to pull in the chapter and pages columns.
  6. Set data validation on the schedule sheet for chapter names in the main schedule section.
  7. Use VLOOKUP to reference book and chapter to get start-finish attributes.

If you are planning to make this schedule and have questions or want templates, DM me! I am more than happy to assist. :) Good luck!
