Finally! A FREEWRITE!

Posted on: Mon 02 September 2024

getting a freewrite. and random updates.

This is my first entry using the Freewrite. After 5 years of following this product, I finally have my hands on one!

The journey started a bit rough when I received this from eBay - a resale market on the internet. I noticed that the device was not able to charge! M suggested that I email the manufacturer to understand how much it would cost to have this device repaired - specifically with a new battery. This way, I could get an estimate of how to best decide cost versus benefit. I am so glad he made that suggestion because I got a response! Not only do they not take repairs, but there's something about a USB-A to USB-C connection that is required for the device to charge.

Well, as you can see, the Freewrite was able to receive a charge and we are now in business.

Instead of purchasing this for $500 (as an early backer) or $649, I got this device for $240 (including shipping.) I figured it would be a low-risk experiment and held onto that logic as the anguish of a potentially mal-functioning battery wracked my brain. I will admit that a part of me was convinced to just get a brand new device and return this one via the eBay Customer Protection policy.

I am so happy this is working! I hope to use it as a way to keep my blog alive - sharing little tidbits of my day in a fun and informal format.

Okay, onto the tidbits...

THOUGHTS ON ARCHIVING INSTAGRAM PHOTOS I have a lot of photos on my Instagram. It is a record of my growth from college freshman to early-30's adult (as of today.) I like my old photos because they were OF A TIME. Now? It seems like people are really focused on a highly curated version of their life. The funny thing is - I was much more adventurous as a young person than I am now. Maybe I want to keep those old photos up as a record of who I used to be.

SLEEPING GODS GAME

I picked up this game because of its easy-to-stop-and-pick-up structure. I guess another way to put it is that it is a legacy game. The mechanics have some DnD-esque parts to it, such as the concept of initiative and armour classes. But it levels the playing field by giving characters a guaranteed baseline of probable success.

Playing with M was great since we agreed to do the walk through after a bit of light studying at lunch. We finished the tutorial and will pick it up again next time.

The only annoying part was packing up the game because 1) I was running out of steam and 2) there never seems to be enough baggies for all the little pieces.

My Trip to Hearst Castle

Posted on: Mon 13 June 2022

A strange encounter at the hotel cafeteria.

I’m here at the cafeteria of the Hampton Inn. My phone went off at 6:30am, but I was already awake. The sun peeped through the windows with the vigour of a midday show.

The plan today is to go to Hearst Castle. A 40 minute drive to the visitor center before the 30 minute winding road up to the property. I’m looking forward to the tour even if we’ve done it once before. My biggest concern is the heat and UV. Yesterday, at lunch, we ate outside and I was sunburned through my top.

Here at the cafe, there is a family celebrating graduation (or similar.) They are a Texan family who boasted loudly of their gun collection and mentioned the mass shootings in the Bay Area - San Jose. The tone was relaxed; a sharp contrast to the low and muted murmur of a community shocked and saddened. “I have an AR-15,” drawled the new grad with a casual, if not praise-seeking voice. Her boy friend smartly replied with a glowing review of their recent shooting range date before sliding past the crowd to grab breakfast. The extended family glowed proudly, confident that the next generation will carry on the family tradition.

As the boyfriend passed, we locked eyes.

For a man coming from a culture that identifies himself as the norm and me, a Chinese woman, as “other”, I could not help but wonder what kind of mental math ran through his mind in that moment. ————— What a strange interaction. These interactions give me pause and a heightened sense of awareness.

Running in the Berkeley Hills

Posted on: Sat 17 July 2021

The poetry of a steep incline.

It was a 5 mile climb up 1,300 feet of elevation. I started out my door at 7:00am with a perfectly ripe banana in my belly and two hydration packs filled with optimism.

There were moments where the incline appeared low. But these deceptions are a guaranteed slow burn on my quads. The Berkeley terrain is a mix of self-sustaining urban farms pregnant with dropping stone fruits and the flittering of squirrels or maybe budgies tossing dry leaves in search of seed. I typically run in the middle of the road this early in the morning - opting for the view of the horizon and an unobstructed panoramic view of my path forward. With my ears free of any buds and my mind given reprieve from the drone of my rumination, I like to take these quiet hours of the morning to embrace the beauty of my city.

I ran upwards into the Berkeley Hills gritting my teeth through the challenge of the improbably steep blocks of Cedar, Shasta, and Grizzly Peak. Every stop for breath came with a barrage of encouraging self-pep talk: “You are strong!” “You’ve already recovered your breathing, great work!” “Long slow distance. Just enjoy this!”

As I neared the peak of my planned route, I noticed the air around me was thick with mist. Visibility was at 20-30 feet at best. This is the morning-dew taking rise to float across the world’s atmosphere. I could not help but deeply breathe in this aspirated volume of atmospheric river. I know some of this air came from Africa, the mountains of China, the snows of the Arctic. This damp smell is a mix of the moss and redwood decay, but it no doubt contains particulate dust rich in the nutrients of the Amazon rainforest.

Some people don’t believe in a higher power. But how can you deny the beauty and might of the life-water that powers the great blue marble we call Earth?
