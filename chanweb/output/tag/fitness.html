The FIRST Presidential Fitness Challenge

Posted on: Mon 15 September 2025

I tricked people to relive elementary school.

It was Sunday on the hottest day of a recent heatwave. Twelve of my unsuspecting closest friends arrived at a local park expecting "Field Games and BBQ." Meanwhile, I was expecting a revolt as soon as I revelaed my loosely veiled aspiration to relive on of the more humiliating experiences of our mandatory Phys.Ed classes in school - the Presidential Fitness Challenge. The goal wasn't to relive the humiliation, but rather to have a structured out-of-doors event that I could repeat year after year.

To the one unitiated adult friend of the group (him being of European nationality), I explained it like this:

In the 80s and 90s in the Unite States, we had something called the Presidential Fitness Challenge where we had some "standard" activities and expectations to be considered fit. I did not tell everyone [attending (sic.)] that the "field" part is this test. Many people have negative experiences in elementary school doing these. So please keep this secret.

The two uninitiated nephews were led to believe you cannot be president without meeting our current president's physical capabilities. No one bothered to correct this misinformation as it later became a bit of a running joke.

Somewhere among the group after the great reveal, I heard the word "redemption" here and there. It really was! Many participants surprised themselves with their current capabilities having exceeded their personal benchmarks set half-their-age-or-more ago, although, perhaps with some residual soreness over the next couple of days.

While performance was the general goal of my guests, making sure everyone had fun was mine. Three and a half hours in the morning sun and six hours in the glow of a few cold bubbly drinks later, I would say we all hit our personal targets and could call the weekend well spent!

Running in the Berkeley Hills

Posted on: Sat 17 July 2021

The poetry of a steep incline.

It was a 5 mile climb up 1,300 feet of elevation. I started out my door at 7:00am with a perfectly ripe banana in my belly and two hydration packs filled with optimism.

There were moments where the incline appeared low. But these deceptions are a guaranteed slow burn on my quads. The Berkeley terrain is a mix of self-sustaining urban farms pregnant with dropping stone fruits and the flittering of squirrels or maybe budgies tossing dry leaves in search of seed. I typically run in the middle of the road this early in the morning - opting for the view of the horizon and an unobstructed panoramic view of my path forward. With my ears free of any buds and my mind given reprieve from the drone of my rumination, I like to take these quiet hours of the morning to embrace the beauty of my city.

I ran upwards into the Berkeley Hills gritting my teeth through the challenge of the improbably steep blocks of Cedar, Shasta, and Grizzly Peak. Every stop for breath came with a barrage of encouraging self-pep talk: “You are strong!” “You’ve already recovered your breathing, great work!” “Long slow distance. Just enjoy this!”

As I neared the peak of my planned route, I noticed the air around me was thick with mist. Visibility was at 20-30 feet at best. This is the morning-dew taking rise to float across the world’s atmosphere. I could not help but deeply breathe in this aspirated volume of atmospheric river. I know some of this air came from Africa, the mountains of China, the snows of the Arctic. This damp smell is a mix of the moss and redwood decay, but it no doubt contains particulate dust rich in the nutrients of the Amazon rainforest.

Some people don’t believe in a higher power. But how can you deny the beauty and might of the life-water that powers the great blue marble we call Earth?
